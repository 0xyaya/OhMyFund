## Product

Build a basket once, schedule recurring USDC purchases, and see what it owns. A user has one Privy embedded Solana wallet, multiple baskets, and multiple DCA schedules per basket. The first user is Yann. The demo must show a purchase completing while the browser is closed, followed by an updated portfolio and transaction receipt.

A fund is a basket strategy, not pooled money or a fund-share token. Token balances stay in the user's wallet. Purchase records attribute holdings and cost to each basket. Other users subscribing to a manager's basket is a later feature.

## Agreed decisions

- One wallet per user; multiple baskets and DCAs. No wallet per basket or custody vault.
- Privy server signing executes offline purchases after the user grants signer permission. Logging in alone does not enable automation.
- A minimal Solana program stores Fund and DCA configuration. The worker executes swaps directly; the program does not custody assets or enforce the worker's swap spending.
- A Fund has a manager, asset mints, weights, version, and `is_mutable`. Mutable baskets can change composition. Setting `is_mutable=false` permanently freezes composition; it cannot be enabled again.
- Updates affect future purchases, not existing holdings. Each execution records its basket version and allocation snapshot.
- Future subscribers will follow updated weights automatically. Subscription discovery, public profiles, permissions, and multi-user management are out of scope now. No platform-admin fund authority is needed for the demo.
- Token-2022 assets are limited to Backpack Securities. Behavioral compatibility checks are explicitly deferred in TODOS.md at Yann's request. No claim of verified compatibility is made.

## Approaches considered

| Approach | Benefit | Cost / disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared user wallet with per-basket ledger | Fewest wallet and funding flows; reuses Privy signing | Requires attribution and reconciliation; selected by Yann |
| Wallet per basket | Separate balances on-chain | Extra funding and wallet setup; rejected because user wants one wallet |
| Program-controlled vaults | On-chain custody and execution restrictions | Much more swap, withdrawal, and security work; deferred |

Privy handles wallet infrastructure; an existing swap service supplies transactions. Build configuration, scheduling, accounting, and the UI. Do not build a DEX, pooled shares, or a new custody system.

## Screens and user flow

Revised UX: [clickable preview](simple.html), [desktop image](simple-desktop.png), and [design system](../../DESIGN.md). The earlier [wireframe](wireframe.html) is retained as history. The revised design abstracts account configuration into two top-level destinations and a fund detail.

1. **Portfolio (home):** total strategy value and estimated unrealized return; shared available USDC; clean fund rows showing value and the next purchase. One primary action: Create fund. No standalone Funds or DCA navigation.
2. **Fund detail:** composition, attributed holdings and returns, and recurring buys together. Add multiple schedules here. Pause/resume is visible; editing, cancellation and archive live in their relevant details. Show the immutable consequence in settings, not a technical flag or version badge.
3. **Activity:** confirmed/partial/failed purchases and receipts across funds. Technical identifiers and basket version are available within a receipt, not the default dashboard.

**Creation:** choose name, assets and weights, then an optional recurring amount/frequency. Default to an editable basket. Put permanent locking in More options and explicitly confirm its consequence. The same flow includes real wallet authorization before automation becomes active. In the prototype, all mutations are local examples.

**Wallet utility:** funding, withdrawal, connection and signing permissions. Keep financial errors and actions required visible where they affect the next purchase. Expose receipt details and accounting explanations on demand; do not hide partial fills or unavailable PnL.

Funding: show the wallet address and USDC balance, with instructions to transfer funds; no fiat on-ramp integration. SOL pays fees and account creation rent for the initial demo. User-initiated token withdrawal remains available through a small wallet action; it pauses automation until holdings are reconciled. No sell/rebalance flow in v1.

Required states: disconnected, no funds, no schedules, pending wallet approval, pending chain confirmation, no USDC/SOL, paused, partial purchase, failed purchase, stale/missing prices, and accounting mismatch. Preserve form values on errors; never display success before confirmation.

## Minimal data model and authority

### On-chain configuration (Anchor)

- **Fund PDA:** derived from manager wallet and a unique fund ID. Fields: manager, name (maximum 64 UTF-8 bytes), `is_mutable`, `version`, `archived`, and up to 8 unique mint/weight entries. The eight-asset cap bounds account size; it does not require executing all swaps in one transaction. Weights are positive integer basis points and sum to 10,000. Demo uses 2–3 assets.
- **DCA PDA:** derived from owner wallet and a unique schedule ID. Fields: owner, fund reference, USDC amount in base units, interval seconds, first-run timestamp, status, revision. In v1, owner must equal the fund manager. Many DCA accounts can reference the same fund.
- Fund manager signs creation, edits, permanent lock, and archive. DCA owner signs creation, edits, pause/resume, and cancel. No manager transfer in v1.
- The program validates signer ownership, PDA seeds, same-owner fund references, account types, allocation bounds, duplicate mints, checked arithmetic, positive amounts, and supported intervals. An immutable fund cannot change composition or become mutable; renaming and archiving remain possible.
- DCA status is active, paused, or cancelled; cancelled is terminal. Editing a schedule increments its revision. Fund composition edits increment its version. Archive prohibits new executions; existing schedules remain historical and are treated as inactive by the worker.
- “Delete” means archive a fund or cancel a DCA. Do not close funded history or erase receipts. Archiving does not sell holdings.

### Off-chain execution and accounting (Postgres)

- Wallet mapping: authenticated Privy user → embedded wallet address and Privy wallet ID. Verify this mapping server-side; never accept a caller's arbitrary wallet ID.
- Schedule cursor: next due slot, revision, claimed execution, and last confirmed outcome. The chain is authoritative for configuration; the database is authoritative for worker progress. Do not put a second mutable `next_execution` cursor on-chain in v1.
- Execution: unique `(DCA address, revision, due slot)`; basket version and full mint/weight snapshot; planned USDC amounts; per-leg attempt state, provider request identifier, signature, actual token/USDC deltas, timestamps, fees, and failure reason.
- Position ledger: immutable confirmed fills attributed to `(wallet, fund, mint)`. Rebuild aggregates from those fills. A receipt insertion is unique by transaction signature and swap leg, so replaying confirmation cannot double-count a purchase.

## Worker behavior

1. Poll due schedules using one durable worker. Claim work transactionally; serialize executions per wallet so schedules cannot race for the same balance. Across schedules, order by due time then DCA address.
2. Refresh confirmed on-chain fund and DCA state. Reject archived funds, inactive DCAs, stale revisions, revoked signer access, or unsupported mints. Snapshot the latest basket version at execution preparation. Recheck configuration immediately before first submission; rebuild if changed. Once a leg is sent, finish or reconcile using that snapshot, never mix versions in one purchase.
3. Split the USDC amount by weights using integer arithmetic. Give rounding remainder to the final asset. Fail clearly if an allocation is below the venue's minimum. Require enough USDC for the full purchase and enough SOL for bounded fees/rent before starting; external wallet activity can still interrupt it.
4. Execute one exact-input swap per asset sequentially. This avoids depending on a multi-swap transaction fitting Solana limits. Build through one swap adapter (Jupiter candidate, exact API selected in the first integration spike), validate input/output mints and wallet destinations, enforce configured maximum slippage (initial proposal 1%), bound fees, simulate, and request Privy signing/sending.
5. Persist a stable attempt identifier before sending. On an API/RPC timeout, reconcile the existing attempt using provider status/signature. Never create a fresh purchase merely because a response was lost. If submission outcome cannot be established, mark “needs reconciliation” and block further automation for that wallet. Provider idempotency/status behavior must be verified during the spike.
6. Retry a leg only after the previous attempt is proven not to have landed and cannot still land. Completed legs are never purchased again. If a later leg fails, show a partial purchase and retain the successful fills. Pause that schedule for review; do not redistribute the missing leg's budget silently.
7. Check pause/cancel/archive before each unsent leg. Already submitted transactions may complete after a pause; the UI states this. A pause during a purchase leaves an explicit partial/cancelled remainder.
8. Schedule intervals are daily or weekly in the UI; an explicit demo mode allows a short interval. No catch-up burst after downtime: process at most one overdue occurrence, mark skipped slots, then choose the next future slot. Insufficient funds skips that occurrence with a visible reason. Resuming starts at the next future slot; it does not accumulate missed purchases.

This provides controlled execution and conservative recovery, not a claim of exactly-once execution across arbitrary external failures.

## Security boundary

- User owns the wallet and grants a revocable server signer. Keep authorization credentials in server secrets and out of frontend bundles and logs.
- Privy policy restrictions must be tested against the actual transaction envelope. A program allowlist alone does not prove amount, destination, or frequency restrictions on swaps.
- The app's basket rules, schedule timing, and accounting checks are backend controls in v1. An on-chain configuration record does not make them enforced on-chain.
- The server signer must not be able to edit user configuration or broaden its own permissions. The first spike must verify that the selected Privy policy can separate permitted swaps from Fund/DCA management transactions. If not, record the limitation and revise the authorization design before real-money use; do not claim user-only configuration enforcement.
- UI pause stops worker execution; removing the signer revokes signing access. Expose both. Simulation and API submission are not confirmation.
- Use limited demo balances. Local/devnet tests first. Real Backpack stock execution needs a separately configured and explicitly authorized mainnet test; no transactions or deployment are authorized by this design document alone.
- Token-2022 behavior checks remain deferred; do not reintroduce them as a hackathon gate. A real stock purchase is still required to demonstrate the integration works, distinct from exhaustive extension/policy compatibility testing.

## Portfolio semantics

- Fund holdings = confirmed purchases attributed to that fund, less explicitly attributed withdrawals. Shared token balances are not assigned to every basket.
- Market value = remaining attributed quantity × current token price. Show price timestamp; missing/stale price produces “unavailable/stale,” never zero or a silently complete total.
- Estimated unrealized PnL = current value of attributed holdings minus their remaining USDC acquisition cost. Cost uses actual USDC debited by fills; separate SOL network fees are displayed separately and excluded from this v1 PnL. No historical performance chart, realized trading PnL, or tax reporting.
- Distribution is marked-to-market weight among the basket's held assets; target weights describe future buys. Assets removed from the basket remain visible as legacy holdings with target weight 0%.
- Unspent USDC and unrelated wallet assets are separate from strategy value. External deposits have unknown acquisition cost and stay unassigned until explicitly handled.
- If actual wallet quantity falls below total attributed quantity for a mint, pause automation, flag affected fund PnL as unavailable, and require manual allocation of the missing quantity across positions. Do not guess which fund lost assets. User-requested in-app withdrawals specify fund attribution and reduce cost proportionally; automated external-transfer attribution is deferred.

## Success criteria and tests

- One wallet creates two funds and multiple schedules against one fund. Unauthorized owners cannot mutate configuration.
- Immutable composition cannot be changed or unlocked. Mutable edits alter subsequent purchases; receipts preserve the earlier version.
- At least one scheduled purchase completes with the browser closed; receipt matches confirmed wallet deltas. Two overlapping schedules do not overspend through worker races.
- Repeated polling, process restart, and lost send responses do not cause blind duplicate buys. Test partial completion and unknown outcomes explicitly.
- Pause/cancel stops unsent work. Archive stops new executions and preserves holdings/history.
- Per-fund totals reconcile to attributed wallet holdings; portfolio total counts each asset once. Test external deficit and stale-price states.
- Meaningful program tests plus worker tests at failure boundaries; one real browser demo. No claim of a security audit.
